p class=lede dropcap
The night the reliquary vanished, the museum didn’t scream. It exhaled—soft, controlled, like a
patron folding a program after the final act.
p

p
On paper, the piece was nothing span class=inline-note data-note=note1an unlabeled bronze
boxspan, archived under a number that didn’t exist in any public ledger. Off paper, it was the
only object in the west wing that ever made the director walk the long way around the gallery.
p

div class=note-panel id=note1
Internal catalog entry “Object 47B. Provenance disputed. Display prohibited. Handling gloved,
supervised, unrecorded.” No donor name. No acquisition date. Just a red mark in the margin.
div

h2 class=article-section-title01 · The Quiet Breakh2

p
There was no shattered glass, no forced lock, no heroic footage of a masked figure slipping through
a skylight. The cameras along the west wing looped a perfect, uneventful hour. Every guard on duty
signed their log. Every door registered closed.
p

p
What we did have was smaller, almost polite a three-minute power dip that never reached the street,
a maintenance ticket closed before it was opened, and a courier manifest that listed one crate more
than the loading bay ever received.
p

p
Artifact crime rarely looks like the movies. It looks like paperwork that almost balances, a
signature that almost matches, a crate that almost weighs what it should. The gaps are where the job
lives.
p

h2 class=article-section-title02 · The Collection That Isn’th2

p
The director called it “the private wing,” though no such designation existed in the floor plans.
A corridor with no public access, climate control that never failed, and a rotation schedule that
left no trace in the museum’s official bulletins.
p

p
Inside, the objects shared three traits no clear provenance, no insurance record, and no label
visible from the door. You didn’t study them; you were permitted to stand near them. For exactly
twelve minutes. With someone watching your hands.
p

p
The reliquary sat on the far wall, alone on its plinth. No spotlight, no plaque. Just a faint
discoloration on the velvet where, years later, I’d still swear something heavier once rested.
p

h2 class=article-section-title03 · The Artifact Economyh2

p
In New Avalon, stolen artifacts don’t flood the market. They surface as rumors a blurred photograph
in a private channel, a line item in an offshore inventory, a whisper that a certain collector is
“buying history, no questions asked.”
p

p
The reliquary fit the pattern. Too specific to fence locally, too recognizable to parade. Whoever
took it wasn’t chasing cash; they were buying leverage. Objects like this don’t just hold stories—
they hold people in place.
p

p
By the time the board admitted the piece was gone, the city had already adjusted. Security budgets
rose. Insurance clauses thickened. And somewhere in the red glow of the riverfront, a new room was
being built around an old bronze box.
p

p
This dispatch is the first entry in a longer file. The reliquary is still missing. The collection
that isn’t a collection is still growing. And the city, as always, prefers its crimes curated,
not solved.
p

const article = document.getElementById(article);
const readingBar = document.getElementById(readingBar);
const readingPercent = document.getElementById(readingPercent);

function updateReadingProgress() {
const rect = article.getBoundingClientRect();
const windowHeight = window.innerHeight  document.documentElement.clientHeight;
const total = rect.height;
const visible = Math.min(windowHeight, rect.bottom) - Math.max(0, rect.top);
let progress;

if (rect.top = windowHeight) {
progress = 0;
} else if (rect.bottom = 0) {
progress = 1;
} else {
const scrolled = Math.min(Math.max(-rect.top, 0), total);
progress = scrolled  total;
}

const pct = Math.round(progress  100);
readingBar.style.width = pct + %;
readingPercent.textContent = pct + %;
}

window.addEventListener(scroll, updateReadingProgress);
window.addEventListener(resize, updateReadingProgress);
window.addEventListener(load, updateReadingProgress);
